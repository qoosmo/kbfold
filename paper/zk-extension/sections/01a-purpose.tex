\subsection{Purpose and scope}\label{sec:intro:purpose}

The object of this manuscript is an explicit zero-knowledge extension of the evaluation protocol of \emph{KBFold}~\cite{MkhidaKBFold2026}. The original KBFold work constructs an encoding of a Boolean-indexed field table using a univariate polynomial in the Boolean-kernel basis. Its normalized fold is exactly restriction of one Boolean variable in the coefficient table. The original paper develops completeness and soundness of its unmasked evaluation protocol and identifies zero knowledge as future work (Section~9, Zero knowledge). In that section the proposed ingredients are a higher-degree random polynomial in the commitment, masking of sumcheck, and masks on the folded words. These ingredients must be analyzed jointly rather than in isolation.

The literature leading to our problem has two related but distinct branches. Multilinear sumcheck and its privacy-preserving variants motivate masking of the intermediate prover polynomials; Libra uses small masking polynomials in a zero-knowledge protocol~\cite{XieEtAl2019Libra}. BaseFold connects multilinear evaluation to folding-based commitments~\cite{ZeilbergerChenFischBaseFold}. In binary characteristic, Diamond and Posen introduce ring switching for packed Boolean witnesses~\cite{DiamondPosen2024}; Kochergin masks its transposition using trace/Frobenius identities~\cite{Kochergin2026}; Frigo and shelat use trace-zero algebra to obtain a bit-packing reduction with linear constraints~\cite{FrigoShelat2026}. Chapter~2 will compare the exact protocol statements and assumptions of these works.

\paragraph{Separation of settings.} The original KBFold codeword-folding implementation uses an odd-characteristic field with a smooth multiplicative domain. Our algebraic discussion of the ring-switching correction instead takes place in the binary extension $\F_{2^n}$. These are two different ambient fields. Statements from one setting are \emph{not} silently transferred to the other. Every later theorem will declare its field and characteristic as explicit hypotheses.
